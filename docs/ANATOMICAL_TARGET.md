# Anatomical target — continuous torso and upper-leg implementation

## Objective

Define the **desired neutral anatomical shape first**, then later fit a fixed-topology quad cage onto it. The old pelvis and thigh construction had stable face indices but unacceptable proportions, a planar crotch and persistent hip/groin folding. Further smoothing and manual offsets were not converging on realistic anatomy.

The target consists of `crates/studio-core/src/anatomy.rs` for pelvis/limb volume composition and temporary preview extraction, plus `crates/studio-core/src/torso_target.rs` for the continuous upper-body surface profile. The editor exposes both through **Anatomy target** mode. It does *not* replace the existing quad generator or export it as a finished humanoid.

## Two deliberately separate outputs

| Output | Geometry | Intended use | Skin/UV/export |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anatomy target | Continuous implicit field; disposable triangulated isosurface | Review neutral anatomical silhouette and volume response | None; explicitly not exportable as a game mesh |
| Topology study | Existing fixed indexed quads and shoulder/hip skeleton | Regression comparison, existing recipe/OBJ/rig pathway | Existing provisional weights and rig remain unchanged |

Default editor mode is **Anatomy target**. Select **Topology** in the viewport for the old quad model. Anatomy mode shows optional named landmark markers and a wireframe of its provisional *triangles*, not quad topology. The top-right statistics distinguish the two modes.

## Data definitions

`AnatomyTarget::from_recipe` creates:
- Landmarks: waist, sacrum, pubic symphysis, left/right hip centres, greater trochanters, knees, sternum, neck top, both shoulders and provisional elbow endpoints.
- A pelvic core and lower abdominal volume.
- A pubic/adductor bridge representing anterior-midline soft-tissue continuity.
- Bilateral iliac, gluteal, thigh core, quadriceps, hamstring and medial adductor volumes.

The lower-body tissue volumes are parameterized ellipsoidal primitives whose approximate distance field is `(length((p-centre)/radii)-1)*min(radii)`. The **torso is not an oversized ellipsoid**: ten ordered elliptical anatomical sections define waist, abdominal taper, lower ribcage, chest, upper shoulder line and neck termination. Their widths, anterior/posterior depths and sagittal centre offsets are smoothly interpolated by height. Tapered upper-arm capsules and deltoid end volumes blend at the shoulders. All components are merged by the same smooth union field. The field is negative inside and positive outside.

This is a **procedural anatomical approximation**, not a biomedical simulator, CT reconstruction or detailed muscular attachment model. Muscles are simplified volume groups.

Morphology dependencies now span overall height, shoulder width, arm length, torso length, chest width/depth, ribcage height and taper, neck dimensions, waist width/depth, back curvature, pectoral and sternum contours, lat spread, shoulder posture/slope, deltoid and biceps/triceps volume, upper-arm taper and girth, and the original pelvic and thigh build controls. Parameter updates reconstruct the anatomical volumes and resample the surface.

## Torso extension: scope and acceptance criteria

The upper-body target is generated by a **tapered, landmark-aware cross-section profile** rather than attaching a second spherical abdomen above the original pelvis. The following regions share one implicit surface:

- Lower trunk and waist merge into the original pelvic soft-tissue volumes.
- Ribcage depth and width vary independently; anterior and posterior depths are not forced to be equal.
- Chest and upper back widen beneath the clavicles then taper into a short neck stump. No head or forearms are implied.
- Pectoral fullness, sternal groove and latissimus width are localized changes to the continuous torso field rather than floating muscle shells.
- Deltoid volumes smoothly join the shoulder to the tapered upper arms, ending near the elbow.

**Visual acceptance** requires a recognisable waist/chest taper, neither a rounded dome nor a detached torso, smooth shoulder-to-arm connections and sensible morphology response under narrow/wide shoulders, low/high body mass and low/high chest depth. The preview is *still only a silhouette target*; a fixed quad mesh, UVs, skin weights and hip articulation are not implemented here.

## Preview extraction

The target is sampled from approximately 0.12–1.76 m vertically and ±0.73 m laterally at reference height, in a three-dimensional grid at approximately **30 mm** intervals. Each cubic cell is split into six tetrahedra. Surface intersections are cached by **shared grid-edge identity** so neighboring cells reuse the same vertex. A signed-field gradient defines outward normals and fixes triangle winding.

A sampled target is a **temporary triangle mesh**. It has no permanent vertex indices or topology correspondence across recipe changes. It must *never* be implicitly converted into a rigged quad mesh or offered as a production OBJ.

Because surfaces sometimes pass extremely close to grid nodes, discarding all very small preview triangles introduced open cracks. A strict near-zero-area threshold preserves connectivity. Replacing this temporary visualization with a robust fixed-topology fitter is a later milestone.

## Validation

The Rust tests in `anatomy.rs` cover:
- bilateral symmetry of the neutral field;
- inside/outside sign checks;
- increasing thigh fullness with thigh-girth changes;
- responsiveness of pelvis, upper-leg, chest and shoulder morphology controls, including named landmark movement;
- continuous negative-field interior from lower pelvis through chest and neck;
- torso chest/waist/neck proportional relationships and arm-to-shoulder continuity;
- preview-boundary clearance for default and combined extreme upper/lower-body proportions;
- finite preview vertices and normals;
- two-face pairing of isosurface edges;
- named landmark and tissue-region construction.

The original pelvis-only field was checked independently. For this **expanded target**, a separate vectorized Python reproduction of the tapered torso, blended shoulder/upper-arm and pelvic tissue fields, sampled within the enlarged bounds, produced **one connected surface** (2,752 vertices and 5,500 triangles using a different isosurface extractor) with positive field values on all six preview boundaries. These numerical results are independent approximations, **not** results from Rust compilation or the native viewport. These calculations are **not Rust compilation or native app visual acceptance**.

No GitHub CI was triggered. The workflow remains manual-only.

## Torso smoothing and anatomical coupling refinement

The ten torso sections are retained, but their heightwise shape parameters
(half-width, anterior/posterior depth and sagittal centre) are now evaluated
with **piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation using shared shape-preserving
tangents**. Previously each segment independently applied smoothstep,
forcing zero slope at every section and producing horizontal bands. The
new interpolation is continuously differentiable at section joins, with
horizontal tangents only at genuine extrema.

The anterior chest-width effect diminishes gradually approaching the
clavicle, while the shoulder attachment also follows upper chest width
by a smaller amount. This allows a relatively narrow shoulder-width setting
alongside increased chest width without moving the deltoid joint inside
an independently widened chest contour. The deltoid is no longer a separate
spherical primitive: it is a smoothly tapered proximal thickening of the
continuous upper-arm volume. The smooth union between the arm and
upper chest has a broader transition region.

At the waist, the lower-abdomen tissue now follows a combination of **waist
width and hip width** and respects waist depth. The pelvis-core volume
is positioned slightly lower, the iliac volumes no longer protrude as high
towards the waist, and the torso–abdomen union is smoother. The existing
thigh and gluteal regions remain available and the old quad study is not
changed.

These are visual-target corrections. No extra public sliders, presets,
regression tests, rigging, or quad-cage fitting were introduced. The
recommended manual inspection is the default character and the broad-chest,
narrow-shoulders, expanded-waist, high-musculature combination, including
front, side and three-quarter views. Native Rust compilation and viewport
results have not been verified in this environment.
